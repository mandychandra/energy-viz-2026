⚡ A quarter century of electricity

US electricity generation hovered around 4 PWh a year for two decades — then started climbing again with data centers and electrification. Underneath that flat line is a complete reshuffling: coal collapsed, gas and wind surged, solar went from a rounding error to a major source, and in the last five years batteries appeared out of nowhere.

☀️ Solar explosion

Utility-scale solar grew from a few experimental plants in the Mojave to one of the cheapest sources of power anywhere — and it is no longer just a California story. Watch Texas, the Carolinas and Florida light up.

🌬️ The rise of wind

Wind went from a few Minnesota and California farms to the largest source of power across the Great Plains. Offshore wind is only just beginning to appear on the Atlantic coast.

☢️ Steady nuclear

Nuclear plants remain the largest source of carbon-free power in the country, running flat out year after year. Zoom in on Palo Verde to compare the scale of one plant with everything around it.

🔋 The battery boom

For a century, pumped hydro was the only way to store grid power at scale, and it still delivers about 24 TWh a year from a few dozen plants. Then, starting around 2020, utility-scale batteries arrived — first at Moss Landing in California, then across the Southwest and Texas — and in 2025 they overtook pumped hydro. Batteries and pumped hydro are separate layers here; both show energy discharged to the grid each month, directly comparable with generation.
