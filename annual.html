What the battery numbers meanMonthly energy discharged to the grid by utility-scale batteries, in gross MWh (EIA-923). These totals are stored energy released, not new generation, so they are excluded from “All generation.” The monthly total is not storage capacity, is not net of charging, and does not include capacity held in reserve for ancillary services. It shows how much energy was discharged, not when or why.

About this view

Annual shows each plant's generation added up over the year; Monthly shows one month. In Annual, circle size and brightness use the average month of the year. Plant labels show lifetime generation. 2026 covers January–August only.

Save image downloads the current view as a PNG. “Large” makes a bigger file for slides or print. Include the credits printed on the image when you share it.

Data: US Energy Information Administration Form EIA-923 (monthly generation, January 2001–August 2026; storage discharge from 2014), Form EIA-860 (plant locations, storage capacity) and EIA-860M (in-service, retirement and planned dates). Data through 2025 are EIA's final release; 2026 is preliminary. For 2026, plants that report only annually are shown at EIA's state-level estimates, allocated by their prior-year output.
Annual figures add up the monthly data, so they are not strict annual net generation. Generation is net of plants' own electricity use (EIA-923). Months when a plant used more power than it generated are negative in EIA's data; they are shown as no generation and left out of totals, which makes totals slightly higher than EIA's net figures (about 0.01% in 2025).
